# Survival of the Fittest

A browser voxel-survival game. You wake up in a crashed ship in the middle of a desert. The wreck is a small, rounded hull with broken wings: a forward cockpit with an insulating-glass windscreen, a central hall with the wreck's crafting bench, a save room whose stern door is the wreck's only opening to the outside, a water compartment whose tank is a pool sunk into the ground, and a regulator room with a pre-installed Heat Regulator. The hull and doors keep sun and wind out, but a sealed space still drifts to the desert's temperature — the desert swings from ~52 °C at midday peak down to ~2 °C at night — unless Heat Regulators hold it neutral. Only the small regulator room starts conditioned. No hunger. Water, heat, health — keep all three in their zones.

## Run it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # dev server
npm run build      # typecheck + production bundle in dist/
npm run preview    # serve the built game
```

The build is fully static — `dist/` can be dropped on GitHub Pages or any static host.

## How it works

- **Bars.** Water drains steadily (faster when hot or sprinting). Low water dims and vignettes your vision; empty water does damage. Body temperature drifts toward the environment: stand under open sky at noon and you cook, stand in the cold at night and you freeze — and inside a sealed space it moves on you three times faster. Body out of the 36–37.5 °C safe zone stops regen; past ~39.5 °C or below ~31.5 °C you take damage. Health at 0 = death.
- **Shelter is physical.** Partial cover is the sum of block thermal resistance straight above you (see `docs/adr/0002-sky-column-shelter-model.md`) — lean-tos, overhangs and insulating glass help. But a fully sealed space equalizes with the outside air: walls alone never save you. A craftable Heat Regulator (one is already installed in the wreck's small regulator room) holds a sealed room at body-neutral temperature — 100 open cells per Regulator, capacity stacks. Sealed means sealed: an open door disables the Regulators and the desert gets in. Aim at an active Regulator and press E to draw on it: your body snaps back to 37 °C on the spot, no drinking required — an idle Regulator does nothing.
- **World.** Infinite chunked voxel desert (1 voxel = 1 m), seeded and deterministic: big rolling dunes, rocky hills and tall buttes, salt flats, stepped mesas cut by deep canyons, round Oasis pools with a bowl-rounded bottom and a living ring of Grass over Dirt — water you can fill canisters from (it is a real liquid: Sources spread and fall, and breaking a Source drains its Flow for good) — oasis pools carry ripe Dune Melon vines — with unripe sprouts beside them that drop Melon Seeds when broken — fruiting cacti and Living Trees, and dead-wood groves stand elsewhere. Empty adobe Towns on a rough 320 m lattice: houses with beds, overgrown Farm Land on real soil, shade trees, and Chests holding the dwellers' seeds — Tree Seeds included — and stores (each cluster's stores are there exactly once). Cacti heavy with prickly pears (and they actually sting when you press into them), and wildlife — desert snakes that hunt anything that steps too close, kangaroo rats that bolt when you see them, Sand Vultures that only dare prey that is already wounded, slow Thornback Tortoises, quick Fennec Foxes, and the deep-Cave Scorpions and Sand Wurms. Cave Mouths where the Caves' band carves up through the crust — stepped sandstone bowls open to the sky, dropping straight into the Caves. A second wreck — and its Engine Core — waits somewhere around 400 m out. On a rough 380 m lattice sit Trader Camps — a windmill tower, a waystation hut, or a farmstead with a water trough — each held by a single robed Trader. Rarest of all, on a 520 m lattice, stand the Grid Relics: crumbling adobe outstations where a Grid of the Dwellers' power age still lives — a roof Solar Panel, Battery and Panel, a line of Power Poles marching to a mast whose Electric Lamp has burned for years — with a Chest of copper rods, salt and one iron rod. A Town's own Grid died where it stood: cold lamps on street Poles, the Panel still in the market shell's wall. A 24-minute day/night cycle drives temperature, sky, sun, moon, and light.
- **Barter.** No coin out here: a Trader swaps goods for goods — your skins, shells, hides, meat and scrap for metal, glass, empty and full canisters, coolant cells and catalysts. Each camp's table carries five offers, fixed for that camp for the whole in-game day and restocked every dawn, so a visit tomorrow morning finds a different five. Stand in front of the stall and press E.
- **Sandstorms.** Without warning the wind rises and the sky goes rust: visibility closes to a few dozen meters, the sun drowns, the noise roars. In the open a storm amplifies whatever the air already is — a scorching noon turns murderous, a freezing night bites harder — sweat doubles and bare skin is scoured. Ship walls, rock shade and the Caves ride it out untouched; out in it, the storm is what kills the expedition, not the day count.
- **Farming.** Press Q with a shovel aimed at Dirt or Grass to till it into Farm Land — sand never holds a furrow — then plant seeds in it. A crop ripens only while water sits within reach — 6 blocks for Desert Wheat, 10 for Dune Melons — and the air in its cell stays in the crop's temperature band: Desert Wheat tolerates 2–42 °C — night, shade, or a tempered room; Dune Melons want 22–45 °C, i.e. the hot open desert. Melons re-ripen on the vine and usually leave a Melon Seed in your hand; wheat cuts back to Farm Land, its sheaf carrying one Wheat Seed and a second about half the time; ripe cacti (anywhere) can be picked by hand for two Prickly Pears. Planting a Cactus pad also accepts plain sand. Aim at a growing crop — or at the Farm Land it stands on, or a young Living Tree — and the HUD names it with its growth percentage and, when it stands still, the reason: no Water in reach, too hot, or too cold; it flips to "ripe" when it's ready. Seeds come from oasis sprouts, Town chests and your own harvests.
- **Wood farms.** A Tree Seed (Town chests, and 20 % of broken Leaves) planted in Dirt or Farm Land grows a Living Tree stage by stage over ~8 minutes as long as water stays within 10 blocks: trunk, branches, canopy. Chopping the trunk yields Wood Log (renewable Campfire fuel — a Log burns away in the Campfire's Coals and leaves 2 banked charges; leaf-scrap Kindling carries half); the seed itself comes back if you pull a sapling. A young tree re-grows parts you break off; a full grown tree stands final — its chopped branches and canopy stay chopped. Cacti also grow: a replanted pad climbs over time and matures into fruit-bearing columns.
- **Water is a substance.** Aim at a still Source with a Bucket (craft: 2 metal at the bench) in hand and place/click to scoop it up — flowing water can't be scooped — then pour the Bucket back out anywhere as a new Source. Oasis pools, your farm's irrigation, and buried buckets of water are all movable.
- **Sacks.** A Sack binds one identical good into a single cell: Sack I (4 dead wood at a bench) holds 10, Sack II (6 dead wood + 2 iron rods) holds 20, Sack III (4 wood logs + 4 cobalt rods) holds 30 — priced in footprint, so a 2×2 item costs four places. Everything you insert — pickup, Shift-click, even machine output — fills the snuggest matching open Sack first; machines and Traders may fill a Sack but never look inside one. Only hands empty one: select it and press **Open Sack** — one in-slot, one out-slot — then **Close Sack**. A Sack never holds another Sack, and a whole Sack feeds no Campfire.
- **Sleep.** A Bed (craft: dead wood + snake skins; spans 2 blocks, one long sleep every six minutes) takes eight hours in the dark to the cool of the morning — a "NAP READY" chip lights when the bed is ready again, and the clock skips to morning.
- **Ship Flight.** Eight **Ship Frame Corners** (bench: 2 Metal Plating + 1 Copper Rod each) standing on a clean rectangle declare a Ship Frame: every cell inside the Frame belongs to the Ship and rides with it — hull, chests, crops, lit thrusters, everything. Put a **Flight Controls** yoke (bench: 4 Metal Plating + 2 Copper Rods + 1 Glass) anywhere inside the Frame and press E at it: the Flight Controls panel reads out mass, engine lift, Rocket Fuel and Grid, and refuses takeoff with the reason (no yoke, too little lift for the mass, dry tanks). Flight burns **Rocket Fuel** (bench: Oil in a Bucket + Coal at the Refinery) through at least four **Thrusters**; at the yoke W/S thrust, A/D yaw, Space/Ctrl climb and descend — and while the engines are fed the Ship holds its altitude on its own: no pilot, no drift downward. The deck is a floor: you can place, break, farm and interact the whole flight, and the crew rides with the hull. Hold Ctrl down to set the Ship back on the ground — landings snap the heading square to the grid, so the Ship lands exactly as it was built. A Ship that runs out of fuel or is too heavy for its engines sinks and settles wherever it came down; unattended Ships stay parked in the air, home travels with them.
- **Loop.** Follow the quest chain (step under open sky → strip the wreck → find an oasis → carry a canister to a beacon → craft a tool → grow and harvest one crop → reach a Town and open its Chest → find the second wreck (the Catalyst drops there) → break free its Engine Core → set it within 10 m of a Save Beacon → light an Electric Lamp on a working Grid → put a machine to work) tracked in the top-right HUD; block-shaped rewards land as crash pods from the sky. Explore, salvage debris and full water canisters around the wreck, dig up buried canisters, campfires (warmth + light, fuel with wood), craft at the wreck's bench — or build your own two-block Crafting Bench anywhere. Eat to stay alive: break cacti for prickly pears (+8 Water each), harvest melons (+30 Water, +5 Health), cook wheat and meat at a campfire (+25 Health cooked), stitch snake skins into Reptile Armour (40% of all damage shrugs off). Fight bare-handed or with any tool, or forge the Metal Sword (bench: 2 Iron Rods + 1 Dead Wood) — far harder hits and longer reach: two swings kill a snake, one kills a rat. Beacons save your run; death stashes everything you carried and everything you wore in a Death Cache at the spot — respawn at a beacon and go retrieve it. Placing the Engine Core completes the Main Quest: the relay calls rescue and you keep surviving.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD / stick | Move |
| Mouse / drag | Look |
| Shift / Sprint button | Sprint (drinks faster) — on touch, the Sprint button toggles it |
| Space | Jump |
| E | Interact (doors, crops, beds, chests, canisters, benches, campfires, beacons, caches, Heat Regulators, trade at a camp stall) — at a Flight Controls yoke it opens the Flight Controls panel, and at the yoke it lets go |
| W/S + A/D (at the yoke) | Thrust / heading; Space / Ctrl climb / descend; hold Ctrl down to land; E, P or Esc leave the yoke; F toggles the Frame outline |
| Q | Use the held item: full canister drinks (+25 Water, −1.5 °C body), food eats; a shovel also tills Dirt into Farm Land; a Bucket at a Source scoops it |
| X | Sweat Surge — force your body cool: −10 Water, −1.5 °C body, 5 s cooldown (needs ≥20 Water) |
| R | Turn a held built block a quarter turn (bench, bed, thruster, walls …) — the bench's far half and the bed's pillow land where you turned it |
| G | Toss the held item away (on touch: the Drop button) |
| Left click (hold) | Mine / break (tool tier matters) — aimed at a mob, attack it |
| Right click / place | Place selected block |
| 1–9 / scroll / click hotbar | Select item — pressing/clicking the already-held one empties your hands |
| I | Inventory — its **Craft** tab lists everything you can make by hand (bench, campfire, wood tools …) |
| M | Maximise the minimap (only with the Surveyor's Visor equipped; on touch, tap the map) |
| P | Pause / resume |
| Esc | Close open menu, skip the guided tour, or pause (save / export world / quit) |

Opening a brand-new world you pick **Easy Start** or **Hard Start**: in Easy the wreck still carries a working Heat Regulator and Sandstorm Breaker; in Hard both are dead **Husks** — breakable for their broken frames, repaired at a Crafting Bench for about half the usual recipe. Either way the wreck keeps no bench and no bunk: hand-craft your first tools, then build the bench. A brand-new world then starts a guided in-world tour: a short flyover of the wreck, then hands-on steps through the hatch, the heat, water, the Chest, crafting, mining, farming and Save Beacons (Esc skips it). The full instructions live under Pause → Instructions (or the title screen). On touch, tapping empty inventory space or panel chrome deselects the held item.

Crafting opens through E at a Crafting Bench; cooking through E at a Campfire — its panel sits inside the Inventory with a Coals box (drop fuel there and it burns away on the spot, its charges staying in the fire), an Input Slot and an Output Slot, and each job costs one fuel charge. Hand recipes live on the Inventory's Craft tab. The active quest shows a waypoint in the HUD — a compass arrow, distance, and a marker beam at the target; a craftable Surveyor's Visor equipped in the Head slot beams every Death Cache, Reward Cache and Chest through walls too. While the Visor is on you also get a **minimap** in the corner: terrain you have walked, Landmarks you know, and your own **Surveyor's Pins** (craft one, plant it with E where you stand, rename it from the maximised map — a pin named "Base" gets its own Visor beam). A **Hidden Landmark Tracker** (Q) reveals the nearest place you have never found and vanishes. The **Catalog** — a button on the Inventory and on every Crafting Bench, Armoury, Campfire and Disassembler — lists every item and block in alphabetical sections and groups recipes by the station that makes them; every entry cross-links everything that mentions it (recipes that make or consume it, the block it places, what drops it) and the links jump between entries. First-time discoveries unlock **Chronicle** lessons — a one-line hint under the quest and a full log in the pause menu — and the Catalog keeps a **Codex**: entries stay locked (greyed, pointing at the Chronicle) until you first hold or craft the thing.

Inventory: drag items between cells; click a cell to select it (Rotate / Drop buttons appear beside the grid, plus Open Sack for a selected Sack and Catalog for anything). An item that spans several cells draws as one tile across its whole footprint — one border, one art, one click target. A **Sort** button tidies the pack — and the open chest, and each Campfire slot has one too — arranging items alphabetically with identical items side by side. Shift-click an item to quick-move it into the open chest (either direction); with no chest open it lifts straight into **Temporary Storage**. A drag released anywhere off the grid parks the item in **Temporary Storage** next to it — the inventory can't be closed while it's occupied; drag the item back from there or use its Drop button. Dropped items pile up in front of you (never inside walls) and need a short beat before they can be picked back up. Three Armour Slots sit above the grid — Backpack, Head, Body: select an item and click Equip — or click the empty slot directly — to wear it; click a filled slot to take it off. Backpack I (6 dead wood + 2 metal at a bench) grows the grid from 5×4 to 6×5 and adds +20 kg (Backpack II: 7×6, +40 kg); a visor's upgrades only work while equipped in the Head slot; Reptile Armour (3 snake skins) in the Body slot shrugs off 40% of all damage. Edible items get an Eat button when selected — prickly pears restore Water, cooked rat restores Health.

Chests are container blocks (craft: 4 dead wood at the bench; the cockpit starts with one). Aim at one and press E: its grid opens above yours, and you can drag items between the two or use **Move to pack**. Chests placed face-to-face merge into one bigger grid — 3×3 per chest, up to three wide, then a row taller per chest (with three chests it's 9×3, four is 9×4…) — and **Chest Connector** rails (craft: 2 dead wood + 1 metal) link chests that can't touch without counting toward the grid themselves. Break one and the grid shrinks; contents that no longer fit spill onto the sand.

Inside the wreck (and anywhere else you build), **Inside Doors** divide rooms for movement and sight — in temperature terms they are just air, and never help seal a space. **Metal Plating** makes interior walls. A sealed room held neutral by Regulators shows a **⚙ REGULATED** chip under the clock.

The **Grid** powers base machinery. Every Grid needs exactly one **Power Panel** — a component without one has zero power — and **Power Poles** reach 10 blocks, linking to other Poles within 16. Wood-burning generators, Wind Turbines and Solar Panels generate; **Batteries** cover the shortfall. Draw over supply trips the panel: the whole Grid goes dark until you press E on it to reset. Machines work purely by adjacency: an **Inserter** pulls one item from the block it faces and pushes it into the block behind (rotate with R when held); a **Planter** plants seeds from the chest below into the empty crop cell two cells up; a **Harvester** reaps the ripe crop directly below and outputs into the chest above. Every Campfire takes fuel into its **Coals**: drop or Inserter-push logs and kindling into the fire and they burn away on the spot, their charges staying banked — a lit fire spends one charge every 10 in-game minutes, so a couple of logs carry it through the night. The standard farm column is seed chest → Planter → farmland → crop → Harvester → output chest. Machines fill the snuggest open Sack in the chest they output into, but never take items out of a Sack — and a whole Sack dropped into the Coals box feeds nothing. A sealed space containing a lit Campfire, a Bed, a working Storm Breaker or a Power Panel is a **Base**: inside one the wind drops to a muffled moan, the fire crackles, the score turns warm and the light closes in — cosy against the edge outside.

Touch: on-screen stick + sprint / jump / break / place / act / drink / surge / item / bag buttons and a pause button (top-left). Sprint is a toggle — press it once to run.
One finger steers the camera — extra fingers are ignored. Buttons fire when pressed; Jump and Break act while held — hold BREAK aimed at a mob to fight it (a Metal Sword reaches farther and hits harder).
In the inventory: tap an item to select it, then tap another cell to move it there (the occupant swaps into its place); tap an Armour Slot to wear the selected gear. There is one selection across pack and chest grids at a time — tap a cell in the other grid to pull the selected item across. Tap the Temporary Storage shelf to lift the selected item onto it or send the parked item back to the grid it came from.

## Tech

TypeScript + Vite + Three.js with a hand-rolled chunked voxel renderer (greedy-ish per-chunk meshing, dirty-chunk rebuilds, fog-tuned render distance). Lighting runs in two modes, switchable in Settings: the default bakes a per-chunk skylight flood (sky dimmed by any cover, doubled cost through Leaves; day→moon ambient and lamp point lights in a custom shader, zero scene lights on the world), or real-time directional shadow maps (see `docs/adr/0003-baked-skylight-with-optional-shadow-maps.md`). The sky carries a sun and moon disc on the same arc, with cool moonlight and stars at night. Audio is fully procedural WebAudio — wind bed, and a generative score that rotates through six genres (drone, arpeggio "wanderer", long-line "nightwind", hand-drum "caravan", plus warm "hearth"/"embers" material inside a Base), each with an instrument voice (saw pad, plucked strings, breathy panpipes, bowed fiddle), a fresh random key and mode every 90–240 s, day/night changing the pools (Music toggle in Settings), with a hearth-crackle layer next to a lit fire; event SFX; zero asset files. No workers, no SharedArrayBuffer, no server. World state is delta-saved per slot in localStorage, with JSON export/import for sharing worlds and transferring saves between machines. Design history lives in `docs/adr/`; vocabulary in `GLOSSARY.md`.
